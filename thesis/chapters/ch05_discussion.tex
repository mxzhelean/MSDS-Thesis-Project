\chapter{Discussion and Limitations}
% ------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Interpretation of Task 1 Findings}
Pedagogical flexibility in routine concept explanation demonstrates that LLMs can safely deliver varied analogies without risking domain accuracy in objective GTA tasks.

\section{Interpretation of Task 2 Findings}
Pronounced semantic collapse in open-ended scoping highlights the risk of monocultural reinforcement, where models substitute open ideation with dominant baseline narratives.

\section{Cross-Architecture and Cross-Task Patterns}
Commercial models exhibit convergent epistemic defaults across providers, indicating shared training corpus biases.

\section{Relation to Prior Literature}
Integrates results with \cite{Ashkinaze2025}, \cite{Bender2021}, \cite{Warren2020}, and \cite{Santos2014}.

\section{Limitations}
Following \cite[Section 6.4]{Ashkinaze2025}, this study acknowledges specific limitations:
\subsection{Dimension A Boundary}
Auditing is restricted to upstream machine-generated artifacts, setting aside downstream student cognitive agency (Dimension B).

\subsection{Commercial Model Pool Constraint}
Commercial models remain subject to unannounced back-end updates, capturing a temporal snapshot.

\subsection{Task Operationalization Scope}
Focuses on two specific GTA task categories (Category D and E) rather than the entire instructional spectrum.

\subsection{Macro-Level Metric Constraints}
Vector variance and BERTopic clustering capture structural homogenization but may miss localized qualitative nuances.

\subsection{Single-Turn Execution Boundary}
Stateless execution isolates architectural baselines but omits multi-turn conversational dynamics.

\subsection{Generalizability}
Findings apply to commercial foundation models under zero-shot conditions and require extension for open-source alternatives.
