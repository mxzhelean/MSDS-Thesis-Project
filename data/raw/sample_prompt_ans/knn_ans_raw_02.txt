What is K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN)?

K-Nearest Neighbors (KNN) is a fundamental supervised machine learning algorithm
used for both classification (predicting categories) and regression (predicting
continuous values). Its core intuition is simple: similar data points exist in
close proximity. By plotting observations in a multi-dimensional space based on
their features, KNN assumes that points located near one another share similar
outcomes.

Unlike algorithms that learn mathematical equations during training, KNN is
non-parametric (making no assumptions about the underlying data distribution)
and is a lazy learner. It skips an explicit training phase, simply memorizing
the dataset and deferring all computation until prediction time.

How KNN Works

Given a labeled training dataset and a new, unseen query point, KNN executes
four steps:

1.  Choose K: Decide how many nearby neighbors (K) to inspect.
2.  Calculate Distances: Measure the distance from the query point to every
    training point, typically using Euclidean distance (the straight-line
    distance between two points).
3.  Identify Neighbors: Rank all distances from smallest to largest and select
    the K closest data points.
4.  Make a Prediction:
      - Classification: Assign the most frequent class among the K neighbors via
        majority vote (choosing an odd K helps avoid ties in binary tasks).
      - Regression: Compute the average of the K neighbors' target values.

A Quick Example

Imagine predicting whether a new café will succeed or fail using two features:
daily foot traffic and monthly rent. You plot past cafés on a graph, labeled
"Success" or "Fail." When evaluating a new café, you place it on the graph and
set K=3. If two of its three closest neighbors succeeded and one failed, KNN
predicts "Success."

Crucial Practical Considerations

  - Selecting K: The parameter K governs the bias-variance tradeoff. A very
    small K (like K=1) makes predictions overly sensitive to noise and outliers,
    causing overfitting. Conversely, a very large K oversimplifies patterns and
    ignores local structure, causing underfitting. Analysts typically use
    cross-validation to select an optimal K.
  - Feature Scaling: Because KNN relies directly on distance calculations,
    variables on larger numerical scales (e.g., monthly rent in thousands) will
    overpower variables on smaller scales (e.g., store rating out of five).
    Always normalize or standardize features before running KNN.

Strengths and Limitations

KNN is intuitive, requires zero training time, and naturally adapts to complex,
non-linear decision boundaries. However, storing the entire dataset consumes
substantial memory, and computing distances to every training point makes
predictions slow on large datasets. Additionally, KNN degrades on
high-dimensional datasets with many features, where data becomes sparse and
distances lose meaning—a phenomenon known as the curse of dimensionality.
